# Platform Ads and Creator Disclosure

Platform policies change often and apply case by case. Open the live policy before relying on this list. Checked 2026-11-01 unless marked.

## Meta

**A1. No income claims in ads.** FAIL on rule C1 in any ad, and on dollar figures near "earn" or "make." The landing page is reviewed too. Meta publishes no banned word list.

**A2. No personal attributes.** FAIL on copy that asserts or implies the viewer's personal or financial situation. Meta lists "Are you bankrupt?" as a violation. "Are you broke?", "Struggling with debt?" and "Tired of being laid off?" fail for the same reason. Fix: describe the service or the topic. "A practical course on automating business admin."

**A3. Destination match.** The landing page must deliver what the ad says, with the same price and offer.

## TikTok

**A4. No get-rich claims.** FAIL on get-rich schemes, MLM recruitment and exaggerated promises in ads and in branded content. Source: the brief's reading of TikTok's North America policy. The live text was not confirmed in this pass, so check it before launch.

## Quebec targeting

**A5.** If Quebec is excluded, confirm it is excluded in the targeting settings. Not visible: NEEDS EVIDENCE.

## Creator disclosure

**D1. Clear disclosure.** FAIL on a paid, gifted or affiliate post with no clear, prominent disclosure. In Canada, Ad Standards guidelines (October 2025) call "#ad" on its own the gold standard. A disclosure buried in a hashtag list is a FAIL. In the US, the FTC Endorsement Guides apply.

**D2. Spoken and visual.** In video, disclose in the content itself, early. A caption alone can be missed.

**D3. AI influencers and avatars.** FAIL when an AI-generated persona gives a testimonial about something it cannot experience, and when it has a material connection with no disclosure. Ad Standards calls labeling the persona as virtual best practice, with tags such as #VirtualInfluencer or #AIcreated. Treat the label as required for this launch.
